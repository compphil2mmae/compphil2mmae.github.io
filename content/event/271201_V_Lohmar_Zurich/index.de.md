abstract: |
  According to pseudo-dominance principles, something is overall better if it is better in some respect and not worse in any respect. Normally, these principles are relativized to certain sets of evaluatively relevant respects, such as individual wellbeing levels or value dimensions, and they are rejected because of problems arising from incommensurateness. This paper argues that these incommensurateness problems can be avoided if the pseudo-dominance relation is not relativized to any particular set of respects. But this comes at the cost of making the pseudo-dominance principle inapplicable. In response to this application problem, two refined pseudo-dominance principles are developed that are both applicable and avoid the problems from incommensurateness. The final part of the paper applies these refined pseudo-dominance principles to population ethics. It argues that they refute the view that the existence of additional happy people is a neutral matter.
