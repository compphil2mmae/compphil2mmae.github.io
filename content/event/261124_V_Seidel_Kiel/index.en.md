abstract: |
  Some people pursue moral life projects: causes that are constitutive of their self-understanding and are morally motivated, aimed at realizing a morally worthwhile end. The success of certain such projects—for instance, the fight against climate change—depends on the moral cooperation of others: on others fulfilling their moral duties. As the prospect of such cooperation to the required degree increasingly fades, those engaged in these projects can be plunged into despair. Using the example of commitment to the well-being of future generations, I would like to examine whether, under these circumstances, one may—or indeed must—say farewell to one's moral project. May one, for example, forget the future, or is hope a duty? These reflections will bring to light an additional reason why those who fail to cooperate morally to a sufficient degree are doing something wrong.
